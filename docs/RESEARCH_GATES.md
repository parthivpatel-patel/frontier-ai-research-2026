# Research decision gates

## Gate 1 — Novelty and feasibility

For each candidate, record the precise proposed contribution, closest prior work, dated references, similarities, distinctions, compute/data requirements, and reasons to reject or retain. An absent search result is not evidence of absolute novelty.

## Gate 2 — Experimental design

Specify hypotheses, baselines, falsification criteria, evaluation datasets, leakage prevention, success metrics, budget constraints, and statistical analysis before collecting confirmatory results.

## Gate 3 — Reproducible evidence

Record executable commands, source revisions, seeds, hardware, dependency versions, raw outputs, failed runs, and negative findings. Do not label planned experiments as completed.

## Gate 4 — Publication readiness

Review contribution claims against current literature; validate calculations and citations; publish code and data where licenses permit; discuss limitations and follow venue-specific disclosure requirements.
