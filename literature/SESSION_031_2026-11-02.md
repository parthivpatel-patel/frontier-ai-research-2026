## New source finding: the upstream dialect may not be conventional LTL notation

A relevant primary source is the original [NL2TL (EMNLP 2023) project README](https://github.com/yongchao98/NL2TL), which describes lifted propositions `prop_1`, `prop_2` and explicitly uses **English-word operators** including `until`, `imply`, and `equal`, as well as interval-qualified temporal operators such as `F[12,100]`, `G[30,150]`, and `U[0,5]`. Its example uses `( ( ( prop_2 until [176,415] prop_1 ) and prop_3 ) equal prop_4 )`. This is a *documented syntax precedent*, not a claim about the actual hidden tokens in the six VLTL-Bench formula pairs.

The [VLTL-Bench manuscript](https://openreview.net/pdf/f16808d741347403f89f359665278fe47be943b2.pdf) states in Section 4.2 that it evaluates the **NL2TL** framework and reuses processed lifted datasets including **Navi** from Chen et al. (2023). The source chain gives a stronger reason to inspect the benchmark's own parser and the NL2TL dialect than to guess more classical operator names. The frozen Session 030 dictionary tested release/weak-until/strong-release/xor/equivalence spellings, *not* the full English-word NL2TL dialect. Zero hits on that dictionary do not rule out `imply`, `equal`, interval syntax, or other valid grammar.

**Important limit:** These sources do **not** establish that any particular one of the 16 unknown word occurrences is `imply` or `equal`. We did not retrieve, reveal, or count the six formulas' unknown tokens, and no parser was extended. This is a newly documented **testable alternative explanation**, not an empirical resolution.

## Why this matters scientifically

A syntax-restricted checker can create a systematic missingness mechanism. If grammar coverage depends on benchmark lineage or operator family, dropping unparsed pairs changes the target population. Any paper that estimates semantic accuracy only on parsable records needs a declared conditional estimand and a separate coverage rate. For an intended unconditional benchmark risk, unparsed cases must be adjudicated or handled under explicit worst/best-case bounds; treating them as either correct or incorrect without evidence is invalid.

4. Compare two independent parser/evaluator implementations on constructed counterexamples; fail closed on disagreement. Report supported/unresolved coverage and maintain the original exact-string and metadata-conditional metrics unchanged.
5. Do not use reference formulas or correctness labels as inputs to a deployed verifier; keep the oracle firewall. Preserve source-family-disjoint splitting and independently adjudicated physical grounding.

**Falsification:** If authoritative grammar does not include these spellings, or the frozen diagnostic finds no matching unknown tokens, reject the dialect explanation. If matches occur but syntax cannot be parsed with independently verified semantics, classify as unresolved. Even full grammar coverage would not prove correctness of the natural-language interpretation or the physics.
