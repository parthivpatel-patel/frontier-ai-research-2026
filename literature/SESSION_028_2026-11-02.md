# Session 028 — Assumption-sensitive LTL witness protocol and prior-art update

Date: 2026-11-02 (US Eastern). **Status at initial commit: implementation submitted; cloud execution not yet verified.** Do not claim new dataset results until the GitHub Actions log is inspected.

## Why this session matters

Protocol 024 showed that 11 of 15 nonexact lifted predictions match their references after proposition-ID alias normalization based on identical recorded grounding metadata. This is explicitly conditional: two metadata entries can look identical while the underlying physical predicates differ. We now test that assumption using small infinite-lasso counterexamples, rather than treating metadata identity as independent semantic evidence.

- [Aggregate-only pinned external audit](../src/vltl_assumption_sensitivity_audit.py): compares unconstrained and metadata-coupled interpretations without printing or redistributing upstream records.

A one-state infinite lasso with p true and q false distinguishes F p from F q, even if a serialized dictionary claims their groundings are identical. If p=q is independently established at every time, the formulas become interchangeable. This is a **constructed mathematical counterexample**, not a measurement of real physical systems or model errors.

## New direct prior-art collision

Wang et al., [VerusEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26464), submitted October 19, 2026, evaluate 13,716 generated Verus artifacts and explicitly distinguish verifier acceptance from specification correctness, behavior correctness, and reference defects. Their published abstract reports that only 42.3% of accepted artifacts pass three semantic checks without a flag. Those are the authors' results, not ours, and their setting is Verus contracts rather than NL-to-LTL physical-system interpretation. Nevertheless, this makes any broad claim that 'formal verification alone is insufficient for generated specifications' non-novel. [Biere et al. (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-49059-0_14) establish that bounded model checking is classical prior art.

The remaining hypothesis must be restricted to **independently validated physical-assumption uncertainty**, with genuine model predictions and source-disjoint adjudication, compared against established formal and conformal baselines.
