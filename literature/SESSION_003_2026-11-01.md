## Narrow testable research question (still unproven and likely overlapping)

Can a *scope-aware claim-auditing benchmark* distinguish three failure classes under controlled physical interventions?

1. **Physical-model error:** simulator differs from physical oracle even when the formal claim is correct.
2. **Formalization error:** a valid proof applies to a formal claim that is not equivalent to the natural-language claim.
3. **Scope error:** the formal claim is true only on a restricted intervention domain, but the reported natural-language conclusion omits that restriction.

A benchmark would pair a natural-language statement, formal predicate, declared scope, executable simulator, and controlled interventions. It would require **positive controls** (true failures), **negative controls** (equivalent physical formulations and correct claims), and **scope-only counterexamples** (outside the stated domain). Labeling is by deterministic oracle, not LLM judgment.

### Mathematical definitions for protocol design (not original results)

Let \(T(u)\) be reference physical dynamics, \(M(u)\) a candidate dynamics model, \(\phi(T(u))\) a formal claim, and \(D\) the declared domain. A physical counterexample exists if \(\exists u\in D: d(T(u),M(u))>\varepsilon\). A claim counterexample exists if \(\exists u\in D: \neg\phi(T(u))\). A scope leak occurs when a certificate is asserted for \(U\supset D\) although its assumptions certify only \(D\). These events are logically distinct; do not conflate them in metrics.

For a fixed budget \(B\), measure counterexample detection by oracle-call count and the rate of false alerts on valid negative controls. Report per-class performance and abstention, not a single aggregated score.

### Pre-registration before any model comparison

- Split by **physical system family** (not only by random seed); tune on development families and report held-out families.
- Fixed oracle-call budgets \(B\in\{8,32,128\}\), and seeds \(0\dots29\), subject to free-cloud runtime limits.
- Baselines: uniform random intervention, fixed boundary heuristic, black-box Bayesian-optimization style search (if feasible), and any published method with reproducible code. No privileged oracle information in the test selector.
- Negative controls must include exact reparameterizations and unit conversions; ensure errors are not due to numerical tolerance.
- Report per-family detection, time-to-first-counterexample, oracle calls, false-positive rates, and uncertainty intervals. No cherry-picking from a single toy family.
- Ablate natural-language context, scope metadata, and intervention feedback **only if** an actual method exists.
- Explicit **kill criterion**: reject the topic if method-level review finds equivalent benchmark or method, or if results show no reproducible advantage over strong baselines.
