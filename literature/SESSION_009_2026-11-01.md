## 1. What changed

We implemented a genuinely trained, tiny multinomial naive Bayes NLP baseline in `src/learned_offset_baseline.py`, tested by `tests/test_learned_offset_baseline.py`. It fits word-count likelihoods from 45 synthetic examples and predicts numeric offsets in English temporal instructions. The five held-out external mutations are not in training. An ablation masks number words before both training and evaluation.

This is a tiny **number-word classifier**, NOT an LLM, temporal-logic translator, scientific reasoning agent, or physics model. The experiment is **exploratory and not publication-grade**.

The outcome is unsurprising: the number word provides the class signal. ## 2. New, particularly close prior art — strong novelty collisions

**A. Nair, Vempaty, Liu & Jagmohan, *Beyond Type-checking: Towards Holistic Evaluation of Formal Specification Generation*, 7 October 2026, accepted NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on AI for Verifiable Coding.**
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10604 ; full text https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10604v1

Read abstract, sections 1–4 and limitations. They explicitly identify the problem of a formally correct proof against a specification that fails to represent the user's intended behavior. They already distinguish formal validity, reference equivalence, behavioral adequacy, required-input coverage, and unavailable evidence, and assemble 350 Lean tasks from VERINA/CLEVER. Their authored control has perfect positive/negative postcondition scores but zero required-input coverage. **Our original headline problem and multi-signal evaluation framing are not novel.** They identify independently reviewed references, checked counterexamples, and matched cross-model studies as future work. Those future directions are suggestions, NOT unclaimed guaranteed novelty.

**B. Suo & Emami, *Quizzing the Translation: A Prover-Grounded Evaluation Metric for NL→FOL*, EMNLP 2026 (October).**
https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.310/

They already use positive and contrastive theorem-prover probes to detect translation errors, evaluate on 434 expert-audited real LLM translations, and abstain on unsupported translations. **Do not claim prover-grounded contrastive semantic probes, labelable error traces or abstention are new.** This is FOL rather than LTL/STL/physics, but the methodological overlap is substantial.

**C. Ahir, *IDRAAK: From Multi-Agent NLP to Few-Shot Prompting for Semantic Drift Detection in Technical Requirements*, 9 August 2026.**
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08801
The public abstract already addresses numerical-constraint semantic drift and compares multiple detection workflows on synthetic perturbations. Only abstract-level verification this session; inspect full text before a narrower novelty claim.

**D. Quansah & Bonnah, *NeuroNL2LTL: A Neurosymbolic Framework for Natural Language Translation of Linear Temporal Logic*, 20 May 2026.**
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22874
Abstract describes verifier-in-the-loop training, satisfiability/nontriviality checks, and minimal-edit repair. Do not present these as new. Full-text inspection and reproduction availability remain open.

**E. Wang et al., *SCP-NL2TL*, 5 August 2026.**
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05439
Selective semantic verification and conformal risk control already exist. We must compare with this method before claiming gains.

## 3. What is still potentially testable (NOT claimed novel)

Narrow hypothesis: When an NLP-to-formal benchmark inherits an unchecked reference formula across semantic-changing perturbations, how do reference-only scoring and independent, physically admissible behavioral probes differ **on source-disjoint real learned-model outputs**?

This is currently a *research question*, not a new benchmark or method. The source dataset explicitly labels its annotations unverified; the discovered mismatches do not imply author misconduct. Physics can separate two formal hypotheses, but without intent-dependent human feedback it cannot identify which the human intended.

The strongest adjacent works already discuss checked counterexamples, intent alignment, and reference quality. Our potential contribution may disappear after close replication and full-text review.
