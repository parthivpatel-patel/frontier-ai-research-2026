## B. Critical identifiability correction

Two predicates can be physically distinguishable without being identifiable as the *human-intended* predicate. Let I be latent human intention, a a physical intervention, D the system dynamics, and T the observed trajectory. If T is generated only by D and a and contains no intent-dependent feedback, then P(T | I=i1,a,D) = P(T | I=i2,a,D). Physics can show where predicates disagree but cannot reveal which one a person meant. Human or independently justified intent-dependent feedback is necessary.

This is an elementary identifiability observation, **not a new theorem**. It corrects the overly strong suggestion that a physical verifier alone could discover an omitted human meaning.

We implemented an exact rational finite-menu engineering baseline, NOT a new active-learning algorithm:
- src/intervention_identifiability.py and tests/test_intervention_identifiability.py.
- Under both restricted toy domains, threshold predicates 4/5 and 9/10 are observationally equivalent; under either wide domain they have exact separating trajectories.
- With invented costs 1 (restricted), 2 (wide reflecting) and 3 (wide damped), the cheapest separating option is wide reflecting.
- GitHub Actions run https://github.com/parthivpatel-patel/frontier-ai-research-2026/actions/runs/41375551637 independently confirmed **64/64 total tests passed**, including ten new tests. This validates engineering checks, not scientific novelty.

## C. Real external-data finding: numeric mutation with inherited LTL labels

**Source:** Michele Chiari and Cristinel Mateis, *Datasets supporting automated requirement formalization from natural language to temporal logic*, released **25 October 2026**, https://zenodo.org/records/23649257 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23649257); source repository https://github.com/michiari/requirements-datasets .

The authors explicitly state that these are candidate data, **not verified ground truth**. The 12 augmented nl2spec CSVs contain 1,790 mutation rows in total and retain the source formula annotations. A separate 1,080-row STL dataset records semantic_validation=not_checked.

For the MIT-licensed WordSwapChangeNumber CSV:
- Upstream commit a38a73a351c07a3d17cb561098485cd4416c5375; Git blob SHA-1 6f6d2e01707e777be9cd0197698f8f19b4381fc8.
- The full 25-row snapshot and upstream MIT license are preserved under data/external/.
- **Five rows** match a deliberately narrow, unambiguous template. The original instruction says b is enabled **three steps** after a, with inherited formula G(a -> X (X (X b))). The mutated instructions say **two, one, four, six, and four steps** respectively, but the inherited formula remains three steps.
- For each row, the infinite trace where a is true only at t=0 and b only at the mutated step k satisfies the mutated instruction but violates the inherited formula under standard discrete-time LTL semantics.
- These five rows represent **one source instruction and four distinct mutated values**, NOT five independent originals.
- **No semantic judgment was made on the other 20 rows** or the other 11 CSVs. No LLM was evaluated.

This is an externally sourced annotation-consistency observation, not a fabricated synthetic example. It is not a claim of author misconduct (the authors expressly warn that the labels are unverified), nor evidence of a novel auditing algorithm.
