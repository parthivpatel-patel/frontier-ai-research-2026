## Revised narrow question

When the *reference semantic oracle* itself admits multiple plausible formal interpretations, how much can an accepted-translation error rate vary across those interpretations? Can an exact physical oracle make this dependence visible rather than hiding it behind a single reference label?

The project should measure the *sensitivity of a risk estimate to the oracle's interpretation*, not advertise the already-known existence of semantic ambiguity or a new conformal guarantee.

## Deterministic finite-sample diagnostic

For each accepted example i, let S_i be a nonempty, explicitly supplied set of plausible reference predicates. For candidate predicate c_i and physical domain D_i, define e_i(s)=1 if c_i and s disagree at any state in D_i, and 0 otherwise. For the current one-bounce toy dynamics, the disagreement test is exact rational arithmetic.

The observed accepted-case error rate for a selection of interpretations s_i in S_i is R=(1/n) sum_i e_i(s_i). Its conservative envelope is

- L = (1/n) sum_i min_{s in S_i} e_i(s)
- U = (1/n) sum_i max_{s in S_i} e_i(s)

For every such selection, L <= R <= U. This follows directly from summing pointwise inequalities; it is **elementary and not novel**. When the choices are unconstrained across cases, both endpoints are attainable; under shared semantic constraints U may be conservative.

A decision threshold alpha yields one of three *finite-sample* statuses: U<=alpha (all supplied interpretations pass), L>alpha (all fail), or otherwise unresolved. These are **not population risk bounds** and do not validate the completeness or correctness of S_i.

### Hand-constructed sanity check (not research evidence)

On a fixed physical domain with reachable next positions [0.5,1], construct three accepted cases:

1. Candidate <=0.8; sole reference <=0.8: certainly equivalent.
2. Candidate <=0.8; possible references <=0.8 or <=0.9: equivalence depends on interpretation.
3. Candidate <=1.0; possible references <=0.8 or <=0.9: certainly mismatched.

Then the exact finite-sample rate can range from 1/3 to 2/3. A single-reference report can conceal this spread. The fixture was designed to produce this outcome; it is not a measured performance improvement.

## Preregistered next evaluation (not yet run)

1. Design a small controlled-language grammar where multiple readings are deliberately enumerated, with **unambiguous controls** and **ambiguity cases**. A reference set is *not* a substitute for independently checking what a human meant.
2. Expand beyond threshold-only statements and one-step reflecting particles to held-out physical families (e.g., damped motion, coupled oscillators), using exact or high-precision validated physical oracles.
3. Separate genuine linguistic ambiguity, annotation disagreement, formalization error, and physical-model error in all labels and reporting.
4. Compare (a) arbitrary single-reference scoring, (b) multiple-reference majority vote, (c) conservative interval reporting, and (d) published ambiguity-aware and calibrated selective translation methods **when runnable without paid inference**. Match information and compute budgets; do not claim superiority based on stronger oracle access.
5. Report ambiguity rate, rate-envelope width, sensitivity of accept/abstain decisions, false-positive controls, and physical counterexample validity. Split by physics family and linguistic template.
6. **Kill criterion:** reject as a paper method if a method-level prior-art audit shows equivalence, if reference sets cannot be independently validated, or if the only result is the elementary [L,U] interval with no demonstrated research value.
