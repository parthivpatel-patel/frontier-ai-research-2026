## 1. Substantive correction to the study's statistical feasibility

Protocol 017 correctly recognized that **59 accepted independent sources with zero observed failures** are necessary for a one-sided 95% upper risk bound below 5%, but only for **one fixed method** under iid binomial assumptions. The main study intends to compare multiple methods and will likely observe some failures. A zero-failure-only formula cannot handle either situation without qualification.

We therefore pre-registered [Protocol 018](../experiments/PROTOCOL_018_NONZERO_FAILURE_AND_MULTIPLICITY.md) before writing code. The new [exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson implementation](../src/selective_risk_gate.py) handles any failure count k among n accepted sources. For k<n, it inverts the binomial CDF P(X<=k)=alpha; for k=n it returns 1. No accepted sources yield undefined selective risk, never zero. Repeated predictions from the same original requirement are aggregated; **any** incorrect accepted prediction makes that source a failure.

For M fixed methods with predeclared thresholds, Bonferroni alpha/M is used for simultaneous marginal upper bounds. With zero failures and target upper risk 5%, the best-case accepted independent-source requirements become:

| Fixed predeclared methods M | Accepted independent sources needed per method (zero failures) |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 59 |
| 2 | 72 |
| 4 | 86 |
| 8 | 99 |

These are **design arithmetic, not measured model outcomes**. Different methods may accept different source subsets, so collecting enough eligible sources to reach each denominator may be considerably harder. With any observed failures, larger accepted-source counts will generally be necessary. Bonferroni-adjusted upper bounds do not constitute a matched-coverage superiority test.

## 3. Closer prior art and implications for originality

The novelty audit found further established work that directly covers selective risk control:

- **Clopper and Pearson (1934):** exact binomial confidence limits. https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats._result_classes.BinomTestResult.proportion_ci.html
- **Geifman and El-Yaniv, NeurIPS 2017:** selective classification and the risk–coverage tradeoff. https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2017/geifman2017neurips-selective/
- **Angelopoulos, Bates, Candès, Jordan and Lei (2021 preprint; 2025 journal):** *Learn then Test*, finite-sample risk control with multiple-testing calibration. https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052 and https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/learn-then-test-calibrating-predictive-algorithms-achieve-risk
- **SCP-NL2TL (2026):** selective natural-language-to-temporal-logic translation with calibration. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05439

Therefore **exact confidence bounds, abstention, risk–coverage tradeoffs, and multiplicity-aware calibration cannot be presented as new contributions**. The plausible research gap is empirical and specific: whether assumption-aware physics-constrained semantic diagnostics improve *independently adjudicated* source-level false-accept risk at **matched coverage**, beyond established grounded verification, may/must, back-translation and selective calibration baselines. This remains untested.

## 4. Limitations, falsification and next work

The exact-binomial interpretation assumes independent source clusters and stable conditional-on-acceptance failure probability under a frozen method and test design. Cross-source authorship overlap, domain heterogeneity, adaptive source selection, test-set threshold tuning, optional stopping or unblinded adjudication can invalidate it. Bonferroni over a fixed roster does **not** repair arbitrary post-hoc method search. A paired method comparison and power analysis still need a separate pre-registration based on lawful pilot data, before confirmatory labels are accessed.

The available external NL2MTL material still has only four original documents and lacks independent source-level human adjudications. No new real-model error rate or model superiority was measured in this session. The next priority is legally reusable, independently adjudicated, source-disjoint natural-language requirements and authentic formal predictions.
