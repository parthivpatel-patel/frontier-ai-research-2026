## A corrected methodological distinction

The existing `src/scope_audit.py` checks finitely many supplied cases. Its `certificate_valid_on_checked_scope` means **sample validity**, never universal validity over an interval. Finite samples cannot justify an unqualified continuous-domain claim.

New `src/exact_domain_audit.py` supplies a deliberately elementary, analytically exact baseline for a one-step reflecting particle, under the restriction `z=x+v+a in [-1,2]`. Rational arithmetic avoids floating-point boundary ambiguity. The image of a rectangular box under the reflection map is computed exactly by evaluating the pre-reflection sum interval at endpoints and included breakpoints `z=0` and `z=1`.

The auditor distinguishes four logically different states:

1. `invalid_certificate`: upper bound fails on the *certified* domain.
2. `asserted_claim_falsified`: certified domain passes, but the asserted domain has an exact counterexample.
3. `asserted_claim_true_but_scope_unsupported`: the enlarged claim holds in this toy physics, but does not follow from the stated certificate.
4. `supported_on_asserted_scope`: the certificate holds and asserted box is a subset of the certified box.

**Truth in a toy oracle is not the same as valid derivation from a restricted certificate.** The baseline can also check whether two scalar threshold translations disagree anywhere in the continuous domain. It cannot establish whether either translation faithfully reflects natural language.

## Derivation (elementary; not novel)

For a box `D=Ix × Iv × Ia`, pre-reflection `Z=[sum lows,sum highs]` is exact because sums of intervals cover all intermediate values. The one-bounce reflection map `f(z)=-z` for `z<0`, `f(z)=z` for `0<=z<=1`, and `f(z)=2-z` for `z>1` is continuous and piecewise affine. Its extrema on `Z` occur at endpoints or breakpoints 0 and 1. Therefore `f(Z)<=t` universally iff `max f(Z)<=t`. For a violating maximum, allocate `z-sum(lows)` across the interval widths to construct an exact rational witness `(x,v,a)`.

This guarantee is **only for the specified toy dynamics and box domain**, not a learned model, unknown physical reality, or natural-language meaning.
